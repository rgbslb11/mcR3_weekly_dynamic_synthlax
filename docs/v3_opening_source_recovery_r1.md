## 1. The short version

Agent 6 established that the 2026 construction reproduces to 1e-12 and that no
2021–2024 component values are mounted. That is a statement about the
filesystem. This lane was asked whether the sources can be *recovered* — from
the repository, from archives, from public authorities, or by replaying a
formula over information that existed before the season.

The answer separates cleanly into two halves, and the separation is the result.

**Two of the four families are recoverable as method.** TrueSkill and Pure
Baxter are fully specified algorithms whose only inputs are prior-season game
results. Litkenhous Layer 1 likewise. All three are gated on exactly one missing
artifact class — a complete prior-season game ledger carrying scores and a
neutral-site indicator — and that class is publicly archived and reachable. The
NCAA scoreboard feed was probed directly for 2020 and serves it.

**Two of the four families are not recoverable at any price.** The Board family
is a human, Chairman-governed instrument first issued in 2026. And Litkenhous —
which the workbook's own Ensemble Parameters sheet lists as an independent
family — is not independent of it: its Layer 2 offseason composite *is* the
Board component vector, reproduced here to 6.7e-16.

So the Board blocker does not cost 25% of the Unified Master Z. It costs 50%.

That moves the finding from "blocked pending evidence" to "impossible". You
cannot acquire a measurement that was never taken. The status is
`EXACT_NOT_POSSIBLE` rather than `BLOCKED` because no evidence-acquisition plan
exists that would change it.

## 2. The exact families, verified from source rather than from labels

Read from `2026_CFB_2026_Preseason_Unified_Power_Ratings.xlsx`
(SHA-256 `4b253545…30807a4a`), sheets `Ensemble Parameters`, `Master Ratings`,
`Data Dictionary` and `Sources`.

The brief's expected names were four: Skill, Litkenhous, Pure Baxter, Board.
Three are right. The first is **TrueSkill**, not Skill, and the distinction
matters because it names the algorithm and therefore the replay path.

| Family | Weight | Master column | Native field | Z column |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| TrueSkill | 0.25 | `F` | `TrueSkill 2026 Preseason μ` | `BM` |
| Litkenhous | 0.25 | `L` | `Litkenhous Adjusted Power` | `BN` |
| Pure Baxter | 0.25 | `AQ` | `2026 Pure Baxter Rating` | `BR` |
| Board I-H | 0.125 | `S` | `Board I-H Power Rating (power_H)` | `BO` |
| Board J-B | 0.125 | `W` | `Board J-B Run2 Power Rating (power_Run2)` | `BP` |

`BQ = AVERAGE(BO, BP)`; `BS = 0.25·BM + 0.25·BN + 0.25·BQ + 0.25·BR`;
`BT = 100 + 10·BS`; `BU = 14·BS`.

Standardization is `(native − AVERAGE) / STDEV` over the 121 FBS rows. The
workbook titles that block "Population Statistics" and then divides by Excel
`STDEV`, the sample deviation — `ddof=1`. Agent 6's finding is confirmed from
the cell formulas themselves rather than taken on report.

### Where each native value actually comes from

None of the five natives is a formula in the workbook. All five are pasted
values, and the `Sources` sheet names their upstreams. Every upstream was opened.

| Family | Externally sourced? | Generated by | Reproducible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| TrueSkill | No | Classic two-team TrueSkill replay, then a published transition | **Yes** |
| Litkenhous | No | Two layers; Layer 1 a least-squares margin fit, Layer 2 a Board composite | **No** |
| Pure Baxter | No | Opponent-adjusted margin ridge, then a 0.70 carry-forward | **Yes** |
| Board I-H / J-B | No | Human component scoring under Chairman rulings | **No** |

Not one of the four is an externally published rating system. All four are
internal instruments; two of them are internal *algorithms*, and that is the

## 3. TrueSkill — a specification, not a table

`Operation_Sythalax_TrueSkill_2025_2026_Thread_Consolidation.xlsx`
(SHA-256 `4b7d84d8…6c7dfc79`) publishes the method completely, on sheets
`01 Method Register`, `10 2025 README`, `21 2026 P0 Params` and `24 2026 README`:

* replay: classic two-team TrueSkill, winner/loser only — `μ₀ = 25`,
  `σ₀ = 25/3 = 8.333333`, `β = 25/6`, `τ = 25/300`, draw probability 0, equal
  game weight, no margin, no home field, processed in published `game_seq`
  order. Parameter set `TS-V0-2025-001`.
* transition: `μ_next = 25 + 0.70·(μ_prev − 25)`,
  `σ_next = √(σ_prev² + 3.0²)`. Parameter set `P0-LAMBDA070-OMEGA300-v1`.

Agent 6 recorded that the chain is initialised at 2025 Week 1 with a flat prior,
which is correct, and concluded that no earlier state exists, which is also
correct. But the brief's §5 asks a different question: whether the *preseason
component* can be computed independently of same-season results.

It can. The 2026 value consumes exactly one season of evidence, starting from a
flat prior. A 2021 preseason value built the same way consumes exactly one
season of evidence — 2020's — starting from the same flat prior. That is
structurally the identical operation, and 2020 results are information available
before the 2021 season opens. It is not leakage and it is not a 25 / 8.333
default; it is the published transition applied to a replayed posterior.

**One caveat travels with it and must not be dropped.** The 2026 value is a
posterior over a *synthetic* 2025 season — `2025_Synthetic_Season_Games.csv`,
757 games. A replay over real 2020 games is a different evidence lineage. It is
reconstructable, but it is not the same object, and labelling it as such would
be the quiet substitution this lane exists to prevent.

## 4. Pure Baxter — a frozen specification with a documented coverage gap

`Baxter_Ratings_v1_Joint_2024_2025_Validation_and_Freeze.xlsx`, sheet
`Doctrine`, publishes `BAXTER-MOV-v1.0-R` in full:

> predicted margin A = HFA × non-neutral indicator + rating A − rating B;
> team ratings sum to zero within each season; margin cap 49;
> ridge λ = 0.3 with the HFA term unpenalized; HFA mode `season_fit`.

and the preseason transition is on the Unified workbook's Data Dictionary:
`2026 Pure Baxter = 0.70 × 2025 Baxter Ridge`.

Coverage is the constraint, not method. `Baxter_v1_complete_metrics.csv` carries
eight season rows — 2006–2011, 2024, 2025 — and the package README states the
same. There is no 2012–2023 fit of any kind, so no fit exists to carry forward
into any season in scope.

`Baxter_Ratings_2024.csv` is the tempting artifact and it is refused for the
reason Agent 6 gave: it is a fit over 2024's own games, and its `games` column
carries post-season counts. It is a legitimate input to a *2025* preseason
value. It is not a 2024 preseason value.

Replaying the ridge over a 2020–2023 ledger is available in principle. One
requirement is sharper than it first appears: `season_fit` HFA needs a
neutral-site indicator per game, and the only historical corpus in the programme
classifies `venue` as BLOCKED.

## 5. Litkenhous — the finding that changes the arithmetic

### It is not the historical system

The brief warns against inferring from the name, and the warning is well placed.
The programme already holds a controlled source-ingestion package for the real
thing, `SRC-LIT-001`, and it is unambiguous:

* the historical Litkenhous *Difference by Score* system was developed by Edward
  Earl Litkenhous Jr. and Francis H. Litkenhous and was an NCAA major selector
  for **1934–1984**;
* the algorithm, coefficients, score transformation, schedule adjustment,
  home-field treatment and normalization are each recorded
  `BLOCKED — not established in the supplied source`;
* `direct_production_weight: 0.0`, and exact reconstruction is a
  `prohibited_use`.

The V3 family's own model card agrees, in its own words:
*"Litkenhous-inspired reconstruction; not an exact reproduction of the
proprietary historical formula."* So there is no historical Litkenhous rating to
find for 2021–2024 — the historical series ended four decades earlier — and the
V3 family is a contemporary implementation carrying a historical name.

### Layer 1 is reconstructable

`2025_Synthetic_CFB_Litkenhous_Inspired_Final_Ratings.xlsx`, sheet
`Methodology`, publishes model `SYTHLAX-LIT-V0-2025-FINAL` completely:

> minimise Σ_g [35·tanh(m_g/35) − (r_i − r_j + H·home_g)]², subject to Σ_i r_i = 0

with one jointly estimated home-field coefficient, equal game weights and no
preseason priors. Pure carryover is then 60% of the prior-season power margin,
recentered.

### Layer 2 is the Board, and Layer 2 is the V3 input

`2026_Synthetic_FBS_Litkenhous_Preseason_Three_Layer.xlsx`, sheet `Methodology`:

> Adjusted preseason | Pure carryover plus an offseason composite from Board I-H
> returning production, portal movement, talent, and coaching continuity;
> adjustment = clip[16 × (composite − 0.5), ±8].
>
> Offseason weights | Returning 0.216216; movement 0.162162; talent 0.108108;
> coaching 0.081081; normalized across their total 0.567567.

Those four weights are the Board I-H sub-weights — 8/37, 6/37, 4/37, 3/37 —
renormalized. Recomputing the composite directly from the Board I-H v2 component
columns, including the academies' talent exclusion, reproduces the Unified
workbook's `Litkenhous Offseason Composite` column to **6.7e-16** and its
`Litkenhous Offseason Adjustment` column to **1.2e-14**.

The V3 native input is column `L`, **Adjusted Power** — Layer 1 *plus* Layer 2.
Without the Board components it cannot be formed at all.

Decomposing the published column: 81.3% of its variance is carryover, 2.3% is
the Board adjustment, 16.4% is covariance. The Board term is a minority of the
dispersion and a total prerequisite for the value. Those are compatible facts
and both belong in the record.

## 6. The Board family — one component vector, weighted twice

The brief asks exactly what the two Board inputs represent. They represent the
same thing.

`2026_Board_I-H_v2.xlsx` and `2026_Board_J-B_Run2.xlsx` carry per-team
`baseline`, `returning`, `movement`, `talent`, `coaching`. Comparing the two
workbooks team by team: **0 of 121 rows differ** on any component. What differs
is the weighting:

| Board | Power formula | Reproduction residual |
|---|---|---|
| I-H | (16·B + 8·R + 6·M + 4·T + 3·C) / 37 | 9.1e-06 (published components are 4 d.p.) |
| J-B Run 2 | 0.35·B + 0.20·R + 0.25·M + 0.10·T + 0.10·C | 1.1e-16 |

The I-H weights were solved by least squares over the 118 non-academy rows and
then confirmed against `Board_I-K_Derivation_Methodology.md` §2, which states
16/8/6/4/3 ÷ 37 outright. The J-B formula is published verbatim on that
workbook's `Rulings Run2` sheet as `POWER_FORMULA`, with `SCENARIO | Run-2
weighting scenario approved by Gary`.

Pearson r between the two power series is **0.99721**.

So the Board family is not a mean of two independent boards. It is one
Chairman-governed component vector under two approved weightings, contributing
one measurement at 25% weight rather than two at 12.5%. The workbook's
`Family Disagreement` column, which treats the Board family as one of four
independent voices, is measuring less independence than it appears to.

### Why no historical version exists, and why none could

Three separate reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Nothing earlier was issued.** A filesystem sweep for board artifacts
   returned Boards I, I-H, I-H v2, I-I, I-J, I-K, J-A, J-B and J-C. Every one is
   2026. No board bears a 2012–2025 label.
2. **The rubric was never written down.**
   `Board_I-K_Derivation_Methodology.md` §7 says so directly: *"A full
   component-by-component scoring manual (every movement/talent/coaching source
   table) is not reproduced here."* No located artifact reproduces it either.
3. **The largest term has no historical referent.** The same document defines
   `Baseline`, 16/37 of Power, as *"Carry-forward strength from **synthetic
   2025** (OSF universe), not real 2025."* A synthetic season has no 2020
   counterpart by construction.

Layered on top are case-by-case human decisions that are not derivable from any
input table — `R-H4-ACCEPT` (NDSU/NIU baseline set to the field median as a
"bounded, accepted risk"), `D-MARKET-NDSU` (a market value corrected by hand),
`R-ECO-01` (a whole component retired). These are minutes, not a model.

**Plainly: the Board inputs are human-created rankings and no historical
versions exist.** Nothing is substituted for them here.

### Substitutes, reported and not adopted

Recorded in `board_substitute_candidates` for later adjudication only. Several
Board components do have real, preseason-dated, publicly archived analogues for
2021–2024: 247Sports Team Talent Composite (talent), published returning
production (returning), transfer-portal team rankings (movement), sportsbook
preseason win totals (market), and SP+ / FPI preseason ratings as whole-board
proxies. AP and Coaches preseason polls are listed with their limitation — 25
ranked teams cannot be standardized over a 130-team population.

The limit is not the inputs. It is the mapping from an input to a 0–1 component
score, plus the rulings that override that mapping case by case. A substitute
board would be a different instrument wearing the same weight, and choosing to
install one is a Chairman decision.

### The key question, answered

> whether all four families are necessary … or whether existing V3 rules
> already permit missing-family renormalization

All four are necessary, and no such rule exists.

* The workbook stores `Included Family Count` as the literal `4` on every row —
  it is not a formula — and its `Validation` sheet asserts
  `MIN('Master Ratings'!BZ5:BZ125) = 4`.
* `combine_unified_z` on the sibling historical-opening-state branch refuses a
  subset with `ComponentUnavailable` rather than reweighting, and documents why:
  a renormalized subset "returns a plausible number computed against a
  population that was never stated, and it is indistinguishable at the call site
  from a complete result."
* A repository-wide search for `renormal`, `missing_famil`, `family_count`,
  `included_famil` and `reweight` across `src/`, `tests/`, `config/` and `docs/`
  returns nothing.

Two of four families being reconstructable therefore does not yield two-thirds
of a Unified Z, or a rescaled one. It yields nothing. No missing-family rule is
invented here.

## 8. Population — reconciled on evidence, no ruling requested

The brief describes the disagreement as **133 vs 128**. That figure does not
appear in any artifact on any branch or anywhere on the filesystem. The figure
in evidence is **118**, from `team_registry_2024.csv` inside the Phase5J
package, and it is what the sibling lane recorded. The correction is registered
in `brief_figure_correction` so a later reader does not go looking for a source
that says 128.

The brief is right that this is an evidence-reconciliation problem. It resolves,
and it does not need the Chairman.

**The two numbers describe two different objects.**

`Synthetic_NCAA_Conference_Distribution_2021_2026.xlsx` is a real membership
record. Recomputed over its bytes it gives FBS 130 / 131 / 133 / 133, and for

`team_registry_2024.csv` is not a membership record at all. Its conference

`GOVERNING_RULES.md` declares *"ECL is a full FBS conference"*, while its

anchor"*. A universe that has to except its own members from reality is a
modelling construct, not a census.

**And the remaining one-team gap has a name.** Kennesaw State began FBS play in

## 9. The one missing artifact class

Everything reconstructable is gated on the same thing, which is why the
acquisition ask is small.

**Required:** complete season game ledgers for 2020, 2021, 2022 and 2023,
carrying per game both participants under a historical identity authority, both
scores, and a neutral-site indicator.

What exists today:

| Season | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | `ARCHIVED_SOURCE_LOCATED_NOT_MOUNTED` | Nothing on the filesystem. The NCAA scoreboard feed **serves it** — probed directly, weeks 01–15 return HTTP 200 with 616 scheduled entries and 523 games `final`; weeks 16+ are empty. |
| 2021 | `ARCHIVED_SOURCE_LOCATED_NOT_MOUNTED` | Raw captures on the unmerged sibling branch; admitted corpus 556 games over 105 teams against a real field of 130. |
| 2022 | `ARCHIVED_SOURCE_LOCATED_NOT_MOUNTED` | 524 games, 101 teams, real field 131. |
| 2023 | `ARCHIVED_SOURCE_LOCATED_NOT_MOUNTED` | 598 games, 114 teams, real field 133. |

Three qualifications, all of which bear on whether a replay would be worth
trusting:

* **The feed publishes no neutral-site indicator.** Both the Baxter ridge
  (`season_fit` HFA) and `SYTHLAX-LIT-V0` (jointly estimated home-field
  coefficient) need one. The sibling corpus classifies `venue` as BLOCKED for
  exactly this reason.
* **The feed carries no postseason.** The 2026 lineage's own upstreams do
  include bowls — the 2025 TrueSkill replay processed 757 games *with* bowl
  games, and the 2024 Baxter fit ran to 14 games for Notre Dame. A bowl-free
  replay is a narrower fit.
* **2020 was the COVID season.** Delayed conference starts, cancellations and
  uneven game counts. A 2021 preseason value replayed from it inherits all of
  that, and saying so is part of supplying it.

Acquiring the class unlocks TrueSkill, Pure Baxter and Litkenhous Layer 1 for
all four seasons at once. It still does not produce a Unified Master Z, because
the Board family remains absent and no missing-family rule exists.

## 10. Decision

**A. Can exact 2021–2024 V3-style opening Unified Z be reconstructed?**
No — not for any of the four seasons.

**B. If partially, which families/seasons are missing?**
`BOARD_FAMILY` and `LITKENHOUS`, in all four seasons. The seasons do not differ
from one another; the gap is a property of the family lineage, not of any
season. TrueSkill and Pure Baxter are missing as *artifacts* but present as
*method*.

**C. Can missing components be reconstructed algorithmically from genuinely
preseason information?**
Partially — two of four families, plus Litkenhous Layer 1, all gated on the
single artifact class in §9. Litkenhous Layer 2 and both Board members cannot,
because the component scoring manual was never written down and the baseline
term is defined against a synthetic season.

**D. Is an exact historical V3 opening state impossible because one or more
families simply did not exist historically?**
**Yes.** This is the operative finding. The Board family is a human,
Chairman-governed instrument first issued in 2026, and through the Litkenhous
Layer 2 dependency it reaches half the Unified Master Z weight. No acquisition
recovers a measurement that was never taken.

**E. What is the smallest evidence acquisition needed to unblock Agent 6?**
The §9 artifact class — one class, four seasons, three families unlocked. It is
worth acquiring on its own merits and it does not by itself produce an opening
state. Whether a two-family opening state is worth anything is a governance
question this lane does not answer, and answering it would require the
missing-family rule the brief forbids inventing.
